ABSTRACT
Opinion analysis (also known as sentiment analysis) is a research area that has garnered
significant attention within both academic and industry communities, due to its potential
to extract meaningful information from user-generated content. This dissertation aims to
develop various approaches to address opinion analysis tasks on user feedback comments,
with the objective of improving performance on Vietnamese datasets as well as those in
other languages. The proposed methods leverage the capabilities of pre-trained language
models and explore multiple paradigms, including classification-based approaches,
generation-based approaches, and ensemble methods built upon language models.
Additionally, the dissertation addresses the challenge of limited labeled data in low-
resource scenarios by proposing strategies to mitigate data scarcity. The dissertation makes
three key contributions, as outlined below:
• Improving the performance for level-based opinion mining tasks: The dissertation
proposes three approaches to improve prediction performance across diverse
datasets: (1) Classification-based transfer learning leveraging pre-trained language
models; (2) Generation-based transfer learning using generative language models;
and (3) Ensemble methods based on language models, which integrate predictions
from multiple model variants to enhance overall performance.
• Developing enhanced approaches for regular and comparative opinion ming tasks:
The dissertation introduces advanced modeling techniques for opinion mining under
both regular and comparative perspectives. For regular opinions, a prompt-based
generative language model is presented within a multi-task learning framework to
jointly extract a quadruple of opinion elements. For comparative opinions, a two-

stage architecture is proposed: the first stage identifies whether a comment
expresses a comparative opinion, and the second stage extracts the quintuple of
comparative opinion elements.
• Proposing methods for addressing data scarcity in opinion mining for low-resource
languages or domains: The dissertation introduces two different approaches to
tackle the challenge of limited training data in opinion mining tasks within low-
resource settings. (1) Leveraging multilingual pre-trained language models in
combination with high-resource language data across various transfer learning
scenarios to boost performance in low-resource languages or domains; (2)
Designing instruction-based prompts tailored for modern large language models
(LLMs) to enable effective learning under limited supervision.
In addition, the PhD candidate also presented the results of a semantic survey study
reviewing previous methods, approaches, and datasets related to the opinion mining task
to identify limitations that need to be addressed within the dissertation's research scope.
